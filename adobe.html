01

Opportunity discovery

The opening I saw

Four signals revealed a credible TikTok opportunity.

The opportunity was not “TikTok is popular.” It was a specific match between the campaign problem, the 13–25 audience, the creative idea and how TikTok drives discovery.

I reframed channel expansion as a testable business opportunity rather than an extra media line.

Business gap

Stronger discovery among prospective young creators without sacrificing the quality of site visits.

Audience

The priority was 13–25: already interested in entertainment, video and creative tools.

Creative

aespa and editable music-video assets gave fans an action—not passive exposure.

Platform

For You discovery and full-screen, sound-on viewing matched the music-first story.

02

Creative fit

Product inside culture

The creative gave fans something to make.

The partnership did more than place aespa beside Premiere Pro. It invited fans to imagine and edit their own music video—turning celebrity attention into a hands-on reason to understand the product.

The product action—imagining and editing a music video—made the message naturally participatory.

01 · Culture

Start with fan behavior

Music-video fandom created a reason to watch, remix and participate.

02 · Product

Make the tool the action

Premiere Pro became how fans could create their own version.

03 · Platform

Match the viewing mode

Motion, sound and participation made the idea native to TikTok.

03

Client influence

From hesitation to a bounded yes

Evidence turned hesitation into a bounded yes.

The client needed confidence on two points: brand safety and whether TikTok could bring qualified Premiere Pro prospects—not only young-audience likes.

I answered both concerns before asking for expansion approval.

Client concern

Would TikTok protect Adobe’s brand environment—and bring qualified Premiere Pro prospects, not just young-audience likes?

How I answered with evidence

Baseline
The prior demand campaign spent $15.9K but produced only 96 engaged visits and a 3.16% engaged-visit rate.
Brand safety
I activated TikTok Inventory Filter and paired it with approved Adobe assets, controlled paid In-Feed delivery and active monitoring.
Qualification
The 13–25 target, aespa narrative and hands-on editing challenge created a direct path from fandom to product use.
TikTok fit
Interest-led discovery and full-screen, sound-on viewing matched the music-first creative better than a standard placement.
Guardrail
Use comparable test spend and judge success on visits and engaged visits—not likes alone.

01 · Reframe

Make it testable

Shift the debate to: can we protect the brand and beat a known baseline?

02 · De-risk

Control the environment

Lock approved creative, Inventory Filter settings and monitoring before launch.

03 · Measure

Define quality upfront

Use visits and engaged visits to separate qualified response from surface engagement.

04 · Close

Ask for a bounded yes

Secure approval by defining the test, risk controls and success criteria before launch.

Decision reframe

I made the client comfortable saying yes to a brand-safe, measurable experiment—not asking them to commit to the channel on belief alone.

04

Proof

The result

Similar spend delivered stronger visit quality.

The approved TikTok test created almost twice the visits and nearly fourteen times the engaged visits—without materially increasing test spend.

The result validated the recommendation on client value, not reach alone.
