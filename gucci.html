Case overviewGucci · South Korea

A one-night show needed a story before it—and a life after it.

Gucci brought its Cruise 2024 show to Gyeongbokgung Palace in Seoul. The opportunity was bigger than broadcasting a fashion event: it was to make a global brand moment feel native to how Korean audiences discover, join and replay culture.

I led Korea-market digital planning and activation across the local campaign journey, translating global direction into platform roles, creative requirements, launch operations and performance learning.

Role
Senior Digital Marketing Manager · Korea digital strategy and activation lead
Scope
Localization, media architecture, creative QA, platform coordination, reporting and post-campaign analysis
Team
Gucci Korea and HQ, iProspect global teams, local media agency, Naver, Kakao, Meta and offline partners
01

Local insight

Prestige had to meet platform behavior

The show could not live on one feed—or one night.

In Korea, search, messaging, social video and livestream commerce form one connected discovery journey. The campaign needed to move with the audience across those behaviors.

01

Discover

Use high-impact local placements and ambassador-led creative to make the Seoul show culturally visible before the broadcast.

02

Join

Turn Naver, Kakao, Meta and city media into coordinated entry points to the live experience.

03

Replay

Keep the show film moving after the event, when audiences had more time to watch, share and revisit it.

Strategic choice

Treat the livestream as the center of a media journey—not as the entire campaign.

02

Media architecture

One story, three audience states

Build anticipation. Own the moment. Extend attention.

The plan assigned a distinct communication job to each phase, then matched platform, format and creative to that job. Local ecosystems carried scale and utility; global social added participation and cultural amplification.

Performance learning

The live event created the spike. Replay distribution created the depth.

Nearly 83% of documented video views arrived in the post-live phase. Kakao native video and Meta post-show content did the heaviest viewing work, while Naver sustained discovery and traffic through premium replay placements.

The takeaway

“A live moment earns attention. A designed replay journey makes it travel.”

For a culture-led launch, the media plan should not peak and disappear. The stronger model is an arc: build recognition, remove friction at the live moment, then distribute the most valuable content when audiences are ready to spend time with it.
